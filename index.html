Three steps, no new phone number

  1. 01

    Forward the line.

    Your carrier sends missed and after-hours calls over. The number on your truck stays the same.

  2. 02

    The assistant answers.

    It says it's an assistant, then asks what the job is and takes the details.

  3. 03

    You get the lead.

    An email arrives as soon as the call ends, and the call goes in a log with a short summary.

Three calls, three outcomes

These are the three calls that decide whether a week was good. Here is what happens on each.

A new job.

The caller wants someone out. The assistant books a slot in your real calendar and emails you the details.

A price shopper.

The caller wants a number. The assistant shares the rough ranges you approved and never quotes a binding price.

A midnight emergency.

No heat, a burst pipe, a dead panel. It goes straight to you flagged urgent, and the caller is told you will call back.

Built for the calls your trade gets

Made for US home-service companies: roofing and HVAC first, plus similar trades like plumbing.

  • After hours.

    Evenings, weekends, and the hours your crew is out on a job.

  • Emergencies.

    Sent to you right away, marked urgent.

  • Price shoppers.

    Rough ranges only. Final pricing stays with you.

  • Warranty and insurance.

    It books the inspection or the service call. Coverage advice stays with you.

  • Spam and robocalls.

    Vendor pitches and robocalls get filtered out of your lead list.

  • The call log.

    Each call is written up: who called, what they wanted, what happened.

Not built for emergency dispatch or life-safety lines, outbound sales calling, or large call centers.

Answered in your callers' languages

Set your line up for the languages your callers use. When a caller speaks one of them, the assistant answers in it. You still read the lead in English.
